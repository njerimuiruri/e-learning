# Tumaini's Climate AI Adventures: covering every side of climate change and AI

**Status:** proposal for review (October 2026). Nothing here is built yet.
**Why:** Episodes 1–3 teach how AI helps people _cope with_ climate change (warn early → act early). They do not yet explain what climate change is, why it is happening, what the world has agreed to do, or what AI itself costs the climate. This plan fills those gaps.

---

## 1. What the series should cover

### A. The science: what climate change is

- **Weather vs climate:** weather is today; climate is the pattern over many years. (Links to Episode 1: Mama's notebook shows the normal pattern.)
- **The greenhouse effect:** gases such as carbon dioxide and methane act like a blanket around the Earth. A thicker blanket traps more heat.
- **The causes:** mainly burning coal, oil and gas (for power, transport and industry), plus cutting down forests and some farming (methane from livestock, fertiliser).
- **The evidence:** 2024 was the warmest year on record, about **1.55 °C** above the late-1800s average, and likely the first full year above 1.5 °C. Long-term warming is about **1.3–1.4 °C**. All of the 10 hottest years on record were 2015–2024. (WMO)

### B. What it means for Kenya and Lake Victoria

- **Floods:** the 2024 long rains killed at least **228 people** and displaced over **212,000** in Kenya (Ministry of Interior, May 2024).
- **Lake Victoria:** in May 2020 the lake reached a record **13.42 m**, flooding lakeside communities. Nyando Sub-County in Kisumu was among the hardest hit.
- **Drought:** 2020–2022 brought five failed rainy seasons in a row in the Horn of Africa, the longest drought in 40 years. Millions of livestock died and over 50 million people in the region faced severe hunger. (WMO, UN)
- **Fairness (climate justice):** African countries have released only a small share of the world's greenhouse gases, but are among the hardest hit.

### C. What people and countries do about it

- **Mitigation, or cutting emissions:** clean energy (Kenya already gets most of its electricity from geothermal, hydro, wind and solar), protecting and planting forests, cleaner cooking and transport.
- **Adaptation, or becoming more resilient:** early warnings, drought-tolerant seed, water harvesting, raised houses, planting at the right time. This is the heart of the current series.
- **Loss and damage:** help for communities after climate disasters that adaptation could not prevent. (For the facilitator notes only.)

### D. What the world has agreed

- **The Paris Agreement** (adopted December 2015, in force November 2016):
  - Almost every country has joined. Kenya ratified it in 2016.
  - **The goal:** keep warming well below 2 °C, and try to limit it to 1.5 °C.
  - Each country writes its own climate plan (a **Nationally Determined Contribution, NDC**) and updates it every 5 years.
  - It covers both cutting emissions and adapting, and richer countries support poorer ones with finance.
- **Kenya's plan:** the updated NDC (December 2020) aims to cut emissions by **32% by 2030**, compared with "business as usual". Its adaptation side builds on the National Adaptation Plan (2015–2030).
- **Early Warnings for All:** the UN's goal is for **everyone on Earth to be protected by early warning systems by the end of 2027**. In Africa, about **6 in 10 people** are not yet covered (WMO). This is exactly what Tumaini does in Episode 2.

### F. The climate cost of AI (the honest side)

- **Electricity:** data centres used about **415 TWh in 2024**, around **1.5%** of the world's electricity. The IEA expects this to roughly **double, to about 945 TWh, by 2030**, with AI the main driver. If that power comes from coal or gas, AI adds to emissions.
- **Water:** many data centres use water for cooling.
- **Hardware:** making computers and chips uses minerals and energy, and old equipment becomes e-waste.
- **Two uses:** the same AI can help clean energy or help find more oil and gas. People decide how it's used.
- **Balance:** the IEA also says AI could help cut emissions in energy, buildings and transport. The lesson is to use AI where it really helps, and power it with clean energy.

### G. Fair and responsible AI for climate

- **Data gaps:** rural Africa has fewer weather stations, so AI knows less there (Ep 5, "The Foggy Map" ✔).
- **Mistakes and uncertainty** (Ep 2 and Ep 5 ✔). **Humans stay in charge** (Ep 5 ✔).
- **Local knowledge matters:** Mama's notebook and Baba's storm records were the data. AI should respect and use them.
- **Access:** not everyone has a smartphone or internet, so SMS and radio still matter.
- **Privacy and ownership:** who owns farmers' data?

### H. What we can do

Follow the Kenya Meteorological Department's forecasts. Keep records like Mama and Baba did. Plant and protect trees. Harvest rainwater. Ask "why?" when an app gives advice. Share warnings with neighbours.

## 2. Proposed changes to the series

### New: Episode 0, "Our changing climate" (about 15 minutes, before Episode 1)

Same pattern as the other episodes (problem → "It's like…" → interactive → lesson → question).

- **Scene 0a, "Why is the weather changing?"**
  - Otieno asks why the rains are different from Grandma's childhood.
  - _It's like:_ sleeping under one blanket, then two, then three.
  - _Interactive:_ add "smoke from coal, oil and gas" to the sky and watch the blanket thicken and the thermometer rise. Remove trees and see less carbon taken up.
  - Real Kenyan examples (2020 lake record, 2022 drought, 2024 floods) as picture cards.
- **Scene 0b, "The world makes a promise"**
  - _Interactive:_ a thermometer with 1.5 °C and 2 °C marks. Sort cards into "Cut emissions" and "Adapt".
  - The Paris Agreement in one picture: every country makes a plan and updates it every 5 years. Kenya's plan is a 32% cut by 2030, plus adapting.
  - Tumaini: "I can't stop climate change by myself. But I can help you prepare, and help people use clean energy wisely." This leads into Episode 1.

### Episode 4: one extra idea

In "Eyes in the Sky", say that forests store carbon. Spotting forest loss early helps **cut** emissions, not only adapt.

### Episode 5: two new scenes

- **"AI's own footprint":** Tumaini discovers it runs on electricity and water.
  - _Interactive:_ power a data centre from coal or from geothermal/solar/wind, and see the difference.
  - Message: use AI wisely and power it cleanly.
- **"What can we do?" (series finale):** the ending brings it all together. A checklist of actions for families, schools and farmers, and a final run through the big message.

### Small additions now (no new scenes)

Facilitator notes in Episodes 1–3 that link the story to the real events and facts above. For example, in Episode 2: "This is what Early Warnings for All aims to do for everyone by 2027."
